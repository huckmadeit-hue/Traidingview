First-Time Tesla Guide

Never driven a Tesla? You’re in the right place.

Ten minutes of reading now buys a completely relaxed trip later. Everything below is written for someone who has never sat in an electric car — no jargon, no assumptions.

Reserve EV orientation video · coming soon

A one-minute Reserve EV walkthrough video will live here: unlock, start, drive, charge, return.

Teslas don’t use a traditional key. Depending on the car, you’ll use a key card held to the door pillar, or the Tesla app on a phone. The door handles sit flush with the door and pop out when pressed correctly — it takes one try to learn.

[Reserve EV’s specific unlock procedure for your trip — key card, app access, or in-person handoff — will be confirmed with your reservation.]

There’s no start button. Sit down, press the brake, and the car is on. Shift into Drive or Reverse, and when you’re done, just put it in Park and walk away — the car shuts itself down and locks per its settings.

Nearly everything — climate, media, trip data — lives on the center touchscreen. The left side shows driving info; the rest is apps and controls. Spend two minutes with it parked and you’ll have what you need. Wipers, lights, and defrost are one or two taps away, and voice control handles most requests.

Lift off the accelerator and the car slows itself, recapturing energy — so you can drive smoothly with mostly one pedal. It feels different for the first ten minutes and second-nature after that. The brake pedal always works exactly like you expect.

Mirror and steering-wheel adjustments are done from the touchscreen (or scroll wheels on the steering wheel, when prompted). Set them before you drive off — it’s under the car/controls icon.

Tap the search bar on the map, type or say the destination, and go. The navigation automatically plans charging stops on longer routes and shows the predicted battery level at arrival — which removes most range worry.

The car’s map shows Tesla Superchargers along your route, with live stall availability. Around Raleigh–Durham you’re never far from fast charging, and most trips around the Triangle won’t need a charging stop at all.

Navigate to a Supercharger, back in, plug the cable into the charge port (it opens with a press on the connector button), and charging starts automatically. No card, no app fiddling. A meaningful top-up typically takes about the length of a coffee stop.

[How charging costs during your trip are handled will be confirmed in Reserve EV’s charging policy — published here once finalized; your Turo trip details govern in the meantime.]

Think of the battery like a phone: percent, not gallons. Around town, you’ll use far less than you expect. Highway speeds, cold weather, and climate use increase consumption — the car’s own estimates account for this, so trust the arrival percentage it shows.

Climate lives along the bottom of the touchscreen — tap the temperature to open full controls. You can also pre-condition the cabin while charging. Using heat or A/C costs some range, and the car’s estimates already include it.

Tesla driver-assistance features are exactly that — assistance. They do not make the car autonomous or self-driving, and they require your full attention, hands on the wheel, and eyes on the road at all times. You are the driver on every mile of the trip. If a feature is unfamiliar, it’s completely fine not to use it.
